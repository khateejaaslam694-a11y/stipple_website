Understanding Color Blindness
What is Color Blindness?
Color blindness is a visual condition where a person perceives certain colors differently from most people. It commonly affects red, green, and blue perception.
Why This Simulator Matters
This simulator helps artists, designers, and users understand how artwork appears to people with different types of color vision deficiency.
How to Use
Upload an image, choose a simulation type, compare original and simulated views, then click any point in either image to inspect the exact color.

Color Blindness Types Information
Protanopia
~1.3% of males
Absence of red cone cells. Red colors appear darker and are often confused with green or brown tones.
Deuteranopia
~1.2% of males
Absence of green cone cells. Most common type. Green-red differentiation becomes difficult.
Tritanopia
Very rare
Absence of blue cone cells. Blue-yellow confusion becomes dominant.
Protanomaly
Mild red weakness
Reds appear less vibrant and can shift toward green-brown.
Deuteranomaly
Most common overall
Greens shift toward red. This is the most common color vision deficiency.
Tritanomaly
Rare
Blue cone weakness. Blue and green can look similar, and yellow can shift.
Achromatopsia
Very rare
Total color blindness. The world appears in shades of gray.
Achromatomaly
Extremely rare
Partial form of achromatopsia with very weak overall color perception.
